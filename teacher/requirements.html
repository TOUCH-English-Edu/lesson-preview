A Whole-lesson rules

Rules that hold on every screen of every Level 2 lesson.

A1 Every page says why it is here

  • Rule: each screen opens with a link to the screen before and says what it is for, in the learner's words. The heading states the point; it is not a label.
  • Why: a page that arrives without a bridge leaves learners lost ("blur"), however good its content.
  • Her words:
    • 「教完 vocab 突然出现这页，学生衔接不到。要有适当的开头，拉回学生知道这页的用意」(21)
    • 「突然教 grammar，和上一页的衔接是什么？title 可以更明显」(23)
    • 「'New sentences: fix the red word' 只是 instruction，要放大这页的用意」(24)
    • 「突然跑到 Kumar，学生会 blur；标题会让人误解」(25)
    • 「大标题和小标题内容表达不够明显，不仔细看其实不知道这页的用意」(64)
  • On screen: a one-line purpose under the heading (ours: about 90 characters).

A2 One idea at a time, in the order a learner thinks

  • Rule: a screen reveals one idea per click: title or question → the case → the task ("Your job: …") → hints and support → answers.
  • Rule: a consequence comes after the replies it follows; a reflective question sits on the side it is about.
  • Why: too much at once, when the page opens, means learners read the hints before they know the case, and the page's point is lost.
  • Her words:
    • 「这页的 slide 不要一开始出太多。出了 case，才出 'Your job: …'，然后才出右边 P.P.F 的 hint」(88)
    • 「可以先出标题，才出 case with doubt，才出 'How would you answer Kumar'」(89)
    • 「'A week later, Siti almost lost RM1000' 这个应该等 Kumar、Siti 他们回复了才出这句」(87)
    • 「你的答案可以一个一个出，不要一次过出完所有答案」(63)

A3 Discussion needs room: answers wait

  • Rule: wherever the class brainstorms or discusses (ours: also votes or tries an answer), the answer shows only when the teacher chooses to show it.
  • Rule: the bare question comes first; prompt cards come only after learners have brainstormed on it.
  • Why: an answer that jumps out ends the discussion before it starts.
  • Her words:
    • 「不要这么快出答案，让老师和学生有空间讨论了才出」(68)
    • 「记得，只要是要老师和学生互动 brainstorm、discussion 的，都不要马上出完答案」(75)
    • 「这个 part（四个 prompt 卡）不要马上和 'What made Mei Ling's warning weak' 一起出先。先让学生 brainstorm 讨论才出」(86)
  • On screen (ours): a "discussion hold" (called "hold" in the rest of this brief): the first press of → only holds, a quiet 💬 cue tells the teacher, the next press shows the answer.

A4 Listening flow

  • Rule: nothing plays when a page opens. One more click plays the recording; the next clicks show the answers, one by one; then the full transcript, after every listening.
  • Rule: stop pauses where it is (it never restarts); every player has a bar to drag back and replay a part.
  • Rule: text in a phone on screen can be enlarged (a magnifier).
  • Why: a small play icon is easy to miss, and then the answers show before anyone has listened; weak listeners need the transcript to see what they missed.
  • Her words:
    • 「学生回答过后，最后还是要出这个 listening 的完整字幕，这样听不懂的学生可以对照」(16)
    • 「WhatsApp 里 grandma 的字幕可以有放大镜」(18)
    • 「icon 太小很容易 miss。到那题按 next 时先播 audio，不要先出答案；有 listening 的，过后都要出字幕」(20)
    • 「任何 listening 都不要一开页面就开始播，而是要多按一次 click 才播」(50, 53)
    • 「所有 listening 请加可以拉进度的功能，不然要一直重新听」(51)
    • 「我一按 stop 请 stop 在那个地方，而不是一按 stop 了，又重新开始」(62)
  • Note: her first notes offered two ways (18: mark the listening clearly, or play it when the page opens; 20, 21: play it first when next is pressed). Her later notes (50, 53) settled it: never on opening, one more click to play. The later note is the rule.

A5 Learner screens carry only the learner's words; the teacher runs the room

  • Rule: how the teacher runs an activity (grouping, timing, "while everyone joins", "no need to explain") goes in the teacher notes, never on the learner's screen.
  • Rule: the screen shows the activity; the way to run it is the teacher's choice.
  • Why: learners do not need instructions meant for the teacher, and a fixed instruction limits a teacher who wants to run it differently.
  • Her words:
    • 「不需要特别注明 'no need to explain'；互动方式由老师安排……'while everyone joins'、'one tap for each message' 这种给老师的普通指令不需要 show，学生更不需要知道」(41)
    • 「活动的方式我们可以给老师发挥」(67)

A6 Vocabulary: no highlighting, except on the vocabulary screen

  • Rule: new words are never highlighted in lesson text. Every new word, and any hard word inside an explanation, has a dotted underline; a tap gives its meaning (her 「按翻译」; ours: EN · 中文 · BM).
  • Rule: the word wall lights up only on the screen that teaches the words; elsewhere the word strip is a quiet reminder to use them.
  • Why: highlighted words read as "this is the point of the page", or "only these words may be used".
  • Her words:
    • 「每个新词汇都要虚线，方便老师解释；里面比较难的解释也加虚线，老师需要时可以按翻译」(19)
    • 「vocab highlight 会让学生以为只有 highlight 的才可以用」(28)
    • 「vocab 不要在 lesson 内容里 highlight，非常混淆，以为 highlight 是重点」(39)
    • 「下面的 vocab 是提醒学生讲英文时多用，不用在 lesson 里特别 highlight，除非那页是专门教 vocab」(37)

A7 Names and colours: formulas and tenses never look alike

  • Rule: formulas are written P.P.F and O.R.E.O, and the screen that introduces one says so in its title ("The formula: P.P.F").
  • Rule: grammar is always "Past Tense / Present Tense / Future Tense", in a colour of its own, never a formula part's colour.
  • Rule (ours, our answer to note 76's 「hint 也让人不明白」): tense forms are shown with what they are for ("finished: get → got"), never as a bare word list.
  • Why: P.P.F's parts are also Past, Present, Future; without "Tense" and a separate colour, learners and teachers mix them up. A bare list reads as "words you must use".
  • Her words:
    • 「PPF 是 formula，可是学生未必知道，标题可以更明显。以后 formula 出现尽量写成 P.P.F / O.R.E.O」(22)
    • 「涉及 tenses 时写 Past Tense / Present Tense / Future Tense，学生和老师才不会混乱」(23)
    • 「这个 past present future 后面要加 tenses，不然会和 P.P.F 混淆。你的颜色也要和 P.P.F 不一样。你的 hint 也让人不明白，会让学生以为是 tips word 要用的」(76)

A8 The lesson must build: each step new, nothing repeated

  • Rule: every stage asks for something new and harder than the last; it is not the same job on a new case each time.
  • Rule: language that is taught is used in the next steps; nothing stands alone.
  • Rule: two screens with the same job are merged and the freed screen gets a new job (57); activities must not feel alike (29, 30; ours: each activity format appears once).
  • Why: when only the case changes, learners feel no progress ("没有进阶的感觉").
  • Her words:
    • 「突然教 because，和之前没有衔接，和下一页也没有衔接或使用，太独立。学了要对课程铺排有用意，或凸显进阶的感觉」(27)
    • 「这个 lesson 的活动太像」(29)
    • 「整个 lesson 的铺排需要优化，没有进阶的感觉，只是一直在换 case situation」(30)
    • 「page 11 和 page 14 活动和用意重复，学生会觉得一样。可以结合在 page 11，这页再设计其他的」(57)

A9 Beginnings: an easy warm-up, then the goal page

  • Rule: the warm-up is easy and low-pressure (for example true or false); no pair work or "tell your partner"; the teacher may ask "why?" (14; ours: one tap, or "this or that", and nobody speaks alone).
  • Rule: the goal page says what learners will be able to do and why it helps them in daily life and at work. It never tells the story.
  • Rule: the goal page uses a framework like her E10 page (slot C3): what learners will be able to do, and how it helps them in daily life and at work; it shows little text at a time.
  • Why: learners are still joining, preparing or new; and a goal page should make them look forward to the lesson, not feel pressure.
  • Her words:
    • 「因为在等着学生 join 课，或者还在准备，或者有新学生还没适应我们的课。所以 pair / tell partner 这种比较挑战的互动可以调成比较简单的，例如猜对、错」(14)
    • 「Today's journey……写的感觉是剧透今天的 lesson。希望是让学生知道今天会学到什么工具，或怎么帮到他们在平时生活或工作……」(15)
    • 「以后的 lesson 这页可以参考类似这样的框架：让学生知道怎么帮到他们在表达能力上的提升，和跟他们在生活和工作有什么关系」(43, 49)
    • 「这页的内容可以，可是设计再优化，全部是字，让学生的视觉感很压力，而不是对这堂课的期待」(61)

A10 Endings: short, safe and warm

  • Rule: the ending has three screens: the lesson summary, a quiet reflection with the teacher's class feedback, and a warm thank-you.
  • Rule: the summary names the ability gained and where it is used, not the verbs and linking words covered; it comes before the ending (82: she moved it to page 16).
  • Rule: no self-rating, no poll of "what helped most", nothing that invites a negative answer in front of the class.
  • Rule: no "your answer helps the teacher plan the next lesson" (lessons are not a series); no "what is still hard for you".
  • Rule: no suggested questions (80); ours: the teacher asks "Any questions?" only if time allows (question E2).
  • Rule: the last page asks nothing.
  • Why: the end decides the feeling learners leave with; a question at the end makes the lesson feel unfinished, and a public rating can make the room awkward.
  • Her words:
    • 「Reflect 是总结，要放更 general 的，让人觉得是能力的掌握，而不是 list 学了什么 verb、什么 linking」(31)
    • 「不需要问 'ask about words, grammar or a scam you have seen'，感觉 lesson 还没结束，不是好结尾」(33)
    • 「可以多 2–3 slide 做收尾……最后再总结今天学的 lesson summary 和用途，对应之前开始提到的 goal」(74)
    • 「（poll）不需要，太容易让学生思考哪个 slide 帮不到我……'Your answer helps the teacher plan the next lesson' 也不适合，我们的课不是连贯性」(79)
    • 「里面不要引导问什么问题，不然每个学生照着问，这堂课不可能在限时时间完成」(80)
    • 「lesson summary 提前放着 page 16 这里」(82)
    • 「这页不需要。很冒险。会让学生故意选 negative 的。让课堂尴尬」(85)

A11 Pictures: faces visible, every picture means something

  • Rule: a speech bubble never covers a face; it may overflow the photo (Marco's notes 12, 13).
  • Rule: a picture must tell the story of that page, or the page uses words instead; a decorative pose with no meaning is removed.
  • Rule: contrast (before / after) is never written on one line (65; ours: two side-by-side panels).
  • Rule: a page should not feel rigid (90), be a wall of text (61) or look half empty (71, 72).
  • Why: learners read what a picture shows first; a wrong or empty picture misleads them.
  • Her words:
    • 「这页的画面感要加」(56)
    • 「这个你要表达 before after 的感觉，可是不明显。写在同一行，学生不知道你的用意」(65)
    • 「这个图无法呈现你要解释的故事，重新设计」(69)
    • 「这页设计太死板，请优化」(90)
    • 「你放这个 Siti 的画面，没有任何意义。请优化配合这页的感觉」(91)
  • Also Marco's notes 12 and 13 (13 came from another device, probably his): a face cut off (「Mr Tan 的头不见了」), and a bubble that covered a face and could overflow the photo to look more 3D. The first rule above comes from those two notes, not hers (question E16).

A12 Online, 1–12 learners, the whole class

  • Rule: every activity works online and in class, for learners of any age, with simple instructions (34, 54, 55; 1–12 learners: the teachers' design brief §25; ours: one or two sentences).
  • Rule: avoid breakout rooms (67); the teacher runs one activity with the whole class (ours: none at all, online in the main room, so one plan works for every class; question E5).
  • Why: we teach online and in groups; time spent explaining instructions is time lost; activities that need a physical room fail online.
  • Her words:
    • 「活动设计要注意我们有 online 课，如果活动要 offline 才可以 pair、才可以执行，要修改。请考虑 group 教学的顺畅」(34)
    • 「这个活动不适合 online group。设计活动一定要考虑流畅度，不要太多指令，学生才容易明白」(54)
    • 「所有 lesson 都要 online 也适合，而且指令不复杂，因为我们有各年龄的学生。不要为了解释指令耽误上课时间」(55)
    • 「你的活动设计操作非常复杂，对小组 online 不友好。breakout room 也尽量避免」(67)

A13 The level feels right: stretched, never talked down to

  • Rule: the language and the tasks must stretch a Level 2 (B1) adult; words a Level 1 learner already uses are not taught.
  • Rule: hints and support give stronger, more useful words, not casual ones.
  • Why (ours): an adult who feels a lesson is too easy stops trusting the course.
  • Her words:
    • 「这个 lesson 整体的感觉比较适合 Level 1，请重新审核」(34)
    • 「词汇要适合 Level 2；deadline、Plan B 这种学生会觉得太容易」(35)
    • 「hint 的内容太随便，必须给更好的字眼，不然 Level 2 学生也帮不到他展开新的 ideas」(40)

A14 Respect the learner's view

  • Rule: an opinion lesson gives equal guidance for Yes and No (both with O.R.E.O); the material never decides the answer for the learner.
  • Rule (ours): before / after and the summary judge the structure, never the side taken.
  • Why: steering everyone to one side is "too subjective".
  • Her words:
    • 「整个 lesson 应该有 Yes 和 No 的 guideline，都用 OREO。现在感觉指定学生一定要解释 YES，太主观」(38)

A15 Hints guide thinking, in separate points

  • Rule: separate ideas are separate bullets, never one comma line that reads as a sentence to copy.
  • Rule: a hint opens new ideas (a reason, a detail, what to do); it does not hand over a finished, mixed sentence.
  • Why: one long line looks like a complete sentence or a chat reply, and mixes reason with how.
  • Her words:
    • 「任何是 point 的要分开写、分点；全部放在一起学生会以为是一个完整句子，会误导。例如：Selling online / - cooking / - photos」(40)
    • 「内容既有 reason 又有 how，又写在一个句子，非常混淆。目的其实是给学生 hint / new ideas 去延展他们对这封讯息的分析（reason / how to do），请给引导而不是全部写在一起，用 point form 比较整齐」(41)

A16 Show the cost of the short message before teaching the better one

  • Rule: before the full model, learners see what happens when the message is short (how the listener reacts), then the full message and its reaction.
  • Why: learners should feel that a fuller message is not "longer for the sake of longer"; it is what helps the listener act.
  • Her words:
    • 「这页可以有一个前后对比：如果 Mei Ling 用短短的 warning，同事们会怎样？然后才有后的对比……学生才会有感受——不是为了讲长而讲，而是这样的沟通可以帮到他们平时」(51)
    • Her sketch for this page: the headline 「People may hear your warning — but will they remember it when it matters?」(64)

A17 The lesson is about a communication skill, not the topic

  • Rule: the lesson's ending widens the topic (scams) to the skill (raising a concern clearly), across industries, roles and everyday life.
  • Rule: a look back shows the whole lesson as a communication lesson: what was noticed, built and used, with the tools learned, not only the story's moments.
  • Why: otherwise learners leave thinking "we learned about scams".
  • Her words:
    • 「要怎么让学生感受到今天学的东西很有用，怎么用在 daily life or workplace？」(30)
    • 「Same skill. Different situations. Once your message is clear, the same structure can work with different people …」(73)
    • Her summary slide: 「One skill. Many real situations. This is not only about scams.」(82)
    • 「课堂应该不只是这四个，这样会让人感觉只是学 scam 和 warming [warning]。可以更完整一点」(92)

A18 Each lesson is built from the overview, not copied from E10

  • Rule: every lesson is built from all the elements of her overview (part G), each filled from that lesson's own syllabus entry (ours: how the elements are filled); E10 gives the order and the job of the screens (C), not their content or their look.
  • Rule: no two lessons feel the same to a learner. The same element is presented differently from lesson to lesson: a different warm-up, a different kind of first case, another version of the vocabulary page, another look for the tense page (G1–G9 say how, per element).
  • Why: a lesson that repeats the scam lesson on a new topic feels like the same lesson again; a lesson traceable to the overview makes one continuous course, whose outcomes do not depend on which lessons a learner happens to book.
  • Source: her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14: the overview part (0:20–24:40), and her close at the end of the E10 part (before 38:30). She calls it a framework, not a rigid template.

B Language standard

Several of the curriculum lead's notes are about the English itself: a model that sounded rude (78), a line that was not a sentence (77), hints that were too casual (40), and wording that was not professional (17). Where she wrote the text herself (B3), we use it as the model. This section is the standard every learner-facing sentence and every model answer must meet.

B1 The register, in one paragraph

Learner-facing English (headings, purpose lines, instructions, hints) and every model answer is (ours, read from her notes below):

  • professional and adult: our learners are working adults on a paid professional course;
  • polite and warm: a model never sounds curt, bossy or rude, even when it gives a firm warning;
  • complete and natural: full sentences a fluent speaker would say, not notes, not telegram style;
  • Level 2 (B1): clear enough for the learner, stretching enough to feel worth learning; no slang, no textspeak, no idioms a B1 learner cannot use;
  • unambiguous: names and pronouns can only be read one way; a story can only be read one way.

Her words:

  • 「这句不像 model answer，不像完整句子，不然有点 rude」(78)
  • 「'The sign: real IT never asks for your password' 这句不像句子，很不自然」(77)
  • 「以后不要用 Home and work，尽量用 Daily life and workplace，比较专业」(17)
  • 「hint 的内容太随便，必须给更好的字眼」(40)

B3 Her own wording is the model of the register

Where she wrote the text herself, it is kept word for word and used as the measure for the rest:

B4 The reviewer's checklist (ours, built from her notes)

A separate language reviewer (not the writer) reads every learner-facing sentence and every model, and asks:

  1. Is it a complete, natural sentence? Notes and short labels are allowed only where they are labelled as notes.
  2. Would a polite colleague say it to a colleague, a customer or an older relative? No bare orders to a colleague ("Tell Ali."), no rudeness, no sarcasm.
  3. Is it adult and professional? No childish wording, no slang ("legit", "gonna"), no textspeak.
  4. Is it Level 2? Clear for a B1 learner, not A2-easy; idioms only if taught or glossed.
  5. Can every name and pronoun be read only one way? Is it clear who did what to whom?
  6. Does a question ask, rather than give the answer? Does a first try avoid handing over a model?
  7. Is it for the learner? Teacher instructions go to the teacher notes.
  8. Are separate ideas separate points?
  9. Are formulas and tenses named right (P.P.F, O.R.E.O, "… Tense")?
  10. Do the facts and numbers agree with the rest of the lesson and with our fixed world?
  11. Is the spelling British / Malaysian (recognise, organise, colour)?
  12. Does every wrong option in a question stay wrong when put into the sentence?

A sentence that fails any line is rewritten before the lesson goes to her; the reviewer writes what changed.

B5 Level 2 word choice

  • A word is too easy for the word wall if a Level 1 (A2) learner already uses it at work (deadline, plan B: hers, note 35; budget, meeting: ours), if it is everyday Malaysian English, or if it is A1–A2 in the English Vocabulary Profile (ours: the test; hers: the examples, note 35).
  • Target B1, chosen because the task needs it; collocations and phrasal verbs often carry the level ("put pressure on", "fall for", "be held up") (ours).
  • Her words: 「词汇要适合 Level 2；deadline、Plan B 这种学生会觉得太容易」(35)

C Slot by slot: the 18 E10 screens as slots

How to read a slot:

  • Job: what the screen is for, in the learner's words.
  • Feel: what the learner should feel.
  • Teacher: what the teacher does (clicks, timing).
  • Reveal: the order things appear, one per click.
  • MUST / MUST NOT: from her notes (ids in brackets).
  • FIXED: the same in every lesson. VARIES: chosen per lesson.
  • Changes with the formula: how the slot changes for P.P.F, O.R.E.O, Recommend or an Optional organiser.

Minutes follow E10 (88 in all; the overview plans about 87). Minutes are ours.

C1 Hook (1 min)

  • Job: "Something many people meet. Today: how to deal with it."
  • Feel: curious; "this is about my life".
  • Teacher: reads the title and the items, then the hook question; one quick yes / no (hands or a tap). No discussion.
  • Reveal: title and items → the hook question.
  • MUST: the overview's hook question for the topic (her overview, every topic has one).
  • FIXED: one title, one hook question, one tap.
  • VARIES: what the items are (E10: three phone notifications at night) and their medium (a screen, a photo, an overheard line, a sticky note) (ours).
  • Changes with the formula: none.

C2 Warm-up (3 min)

  • Job: "Three quick ones: which would you trust?"
  • Feel: easy, no pressure; a latecomer can join on item 2.
  • Teacher: reads each item; the class votes (tap, thumbs); holds until most have voted; shows the answer; may ask "why?"; how much to discuss is the teacher's choice.
  • Reveal: item → vote (hold) → the answer, with a hint card of ideas.
  • MUST: easy, for example true or false (14); ours: each item answered in one quick move (a tap, a vote, a word), about the topic, not the formula. Hints in separate points, grouped (Reason / What to do) (41).
  • MUST NOT: pair talk or "tell your partner" (14); teacher instructions on screen ("while everyone joins", "no need to explain") (41).
  • FIXED: low pressure, each item answered in one quick move; the hold before each answer; hint cards in point form (E10: one tap per item).
  • VARIES: the items and the hint groups' names (ours: e.g. an opinion lesson may use "Helps because / Worries because").
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (E10 part): the warm-up's format itself changes in every lesson, since it is the learners' first impression; it stays low-pressure (for example true or false, a short sentence, a reaction to a picture, a recent experience) (G7).
  • Changes with the formula: none.

C3 Today's communication goal (2 min)

  • Job: "What I will be able to do after today, and why it helps me."
  • Feel: looking forward to the lesson, not pressure.
  • Teacher: reads the headline, one skill per click, then why; under two minutes; no activity.
  • Reveal: headline → skill 1 → skill 2 → skill 3 → why (two cards).
  • MUST: a framework like her E10 page (43, 49: 「以后的 lesson 这页可以参考类似这样的框架」): "TODAY'S COMMUNICATION GOAL" · the goal (an ability — so that the listener …) · "After this lesson, you will practise how to:" three skills with an icon and one line each · "Why this skill is useful": "For your communication" and "In real situations" (daily life or at work).
  • MUST: abilities and tools, never the story (15); "daily life and workplace", never "home and work" (17); little text at a time, visual (61).
  • MUST NOT: a wall of text (61); names or events from the story (15).
  • FIXED: her structure (goal → three "practise how to" skills → "Why this skill is useful") and its headings (ours: kept the same in every lesson; question E10).
  • VARIES: the goal line, the three skills and the two "why" lines, written for the lesson's function and can-do (from the overview).
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (E10 part): the page gives the general goals and why the skill matters (for learners who arrive feeling negative); its content is similar in every lesson, but it should not repeat the same look (G1, G9).
  • Changes with the formula: in E10 the three skills map to P.P.F (PAST tells what happened; ⚠️ and 💬 are PRESENT; 👉 is FUTURE, linked on C9). An O.R.E.O lesson has four parts: see question E6.

C4 First try (5 min)

  • Job: "Answer as well as you can now. We'll come back to this at the end."
  • Feel: "I can't say this well yet" — felt in their own answer, before any teaching.
  • Teacher: plays the message (it does not play by itself); asks each question, shows each answer; shows the transcript; learners write or say their own reply (2 min); reads two aloud with permission; no correction.
  • Reveal: questions → play → answer 1 … 4 (one per click) → transcript → the question to answer.
  • MUST: (ours) a first case the learner answers in their own words, with no starters; the reply prompt is a question ("What will you reply?") (18). When the case is a listening (E10: a voice message): the transcript after the answers (16, 20); a magnifier on the phone's text (18).
  • MUST NOT: auto-play (50); a model answer in the prompt (18).
  • FIXED: the case → the learner's own reply; the same question returns on C14 (E10: listen → answers one by one → transcript → own reply).
  • VARIES: who asks and how (E10: Grandma's voice message at 9:42 pm; others: a call, a request at the counter, a photo pushed across a table).
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (E10 part): the first case takes different formats: not always a phone call, and not every lesson starts with a listening. What matters is the learner's real "before", compared later with the "after" (C14). So a listening is E10's choice, not a rule for every lesson (G7).
  • Changes with the formula: none.

C5 Word wall (4 min)

  • Job: "These are the words my answer needed."
  • Feel: "new words I will really use", not "words I already know".
  • Teacher: says each word, asks what it means, flips the card, the class reads the example together.
  • Reveal: one card per click.
  • MUST: every new word dotted, with its meaning on a tap (EN · 中文 · BM) (19); words right for Level 2 (35).
  • MUST NOT: words a Level 1 learner knows (35).
  • FIXED: this is the only screen where the words light up (37, 39).
  • VARIES: the words (ours: eight, B1, each needed by the task).
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (E10 part): the presentation; 3–4 versions of this page are enough, used in turn, and more visual (G7).
  • Changes with the formula: none.

C6 Word review (3 min)

  • Job: "Quick check: which of today's words fits?"
  • Feel: fast, a little competitive.
  • Teacher: reads each situation; teams call out the word; reveal; a listening item plays first.
  • Reveal: situation → the word (one per click); a call plays on → before its answer, then its text.
  • MUST: the heading says it is a review of the words; separate situations that do not read as one story (36); a listening item plays before its answer, with its text after (20).
  • MUST NOT: messages that look connected, so learners think they must follow a story (36).
  • FIXED: a short review of the words, said as a review.
  • VARIES: the format (ours: a team game, a match, a quick sort; each format once per lesson, A8).
  • Changes with the formula: none.

C7 The cost of a short message (6 min)

  • Job: "People may hear a short message — but will it help them when it matters?"
  • Feel: "a short message is not enough, and I can see why."
  • Teacher: reads the case; the class guesses; the bare question goes up and the class talks (1–2 min); then the prompt questions; then the answers one by one; the closing line.
  • Reveal: the headline question → the short message → the replies ("it looked like everyone understood") → later: what happened → the question → the brainstorm question alone (hold) → the prompts (hold) → the answers, one per click → the closing line.
  • MUST: her sketch (64): a question as the headline; then → later, side by side; a brainstorm strip; a footer line that restates the goal.
  • MUST: answers wait for the discussion (68, 75); the prompts come after the bare question (86); a bridge from the words (21); the cost of the short message is visible (51).
  • MUST NOT: answers under the questions before the talk (75); the page's point hidden in a label (64).
  • FIXED: short message → the listener's reaction → the cost → "what was missing?" brainstorm.
  • VARIES: the case, its medium and layout (ours: a team chat, an email thread, a sticky note, a voice message; not E10's chat-and-photo every time).
  • Changes with the formula: the missing parts are the formula's parts (P.P.F: no example, no signs, no next step; O.R.E.O: no reason, no example, no clear view; a guide: no check, no exact place) (ours).

C8 The full model, and Before | After (7 min)

  • Job: "Same person, another try. Listen: what is different this time?"
  • Feel: "now I hear what a clear message sounds like, and what it does for the listener."
  • Teacher: plays the model; asks and shows the answers one by one; the transcript; then Before and After as two panels; asks how they are different.
  • Reveal: questions → play → answers (one per click) → transcript → BEFORE: the short message → its replies → the result → AFTER: the full message → its replies → the result → the question, on the After side.
  • MUST: Before and After never on one line (65; ours: two separate, labelled panels); each result after its replies; the question on the After side (87); the listening flow (A4).
  • MUST NOT: before and after on one line (65).
  • FIXED: one listening, then the contrast with the listener's reactions.
  • VARIES: the model's speaker and case.
  • Changes with the formula: an opinion lesson gives two models, one for each side, with the same parts (38); a Recommend lesson may give two different picks that both fit the need (ours).

C9 The formula (4 min)

  • Job: "What she did the second time has a name."
  • Feel: "a tool I can reuse."
  • Teacher: names the formula; shows each part with its sentence from the model; links the parts to the three skills on the goal page; the class labels sentences on the phone.
  • Reveal: the formula's name → each part (one per click) → the link to the goal page.
  • MUST: the title says it is a formula: "The formula: P.P.F" (22); formulas written with dots (22).
  • FIXED: name → parts in order → the link to the goal page.
  • VARIES: the formula, from the overview.
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (overview part; E10 part): the formula is reintroduced in every lesson; its names and their use stay the same, but its keywords and its presentation change with the topic, and the teacher's guidance matters here (G2).

C10 The language that makes it work (6 min)

  • Job: "To say each part, you need the right form."
  • Feel: "the grammar serves the parts I just learned."
  • Teacher: bridges from the formula; reads forms and examples; the class fixes each red word (hold); shows the right sentence; phones check one item per part.
  • Reveal: part 1 forms → wrong sentences (hold) → right sentences → part 2 … → part 3 ….
  • MUST: a bridge from the formula (23); "Past Tense / Present Tense / Future Tense" in full, in their own colour (23, 76); the page's purpose, not only an instruction (24); forms shown with what they are for (76).

  • VARIES: the forms, from the overview's time control and its two support structures.
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (overview part; E10 part): the tense page does not look the same in every lesson; the grammar comes from sentences, not sentences from the grammar, with only a small explanation; E10's fix-the-red-word correction is one way among many (G3).
  • Changes with the formula: P.P.F maps one tense to each part. O.R.E.O lessons (Present and Future) teach the overview's support structures instead (O07: will / won't, more and more … are -ing; R11: the advantage of + -ing, might; O02: might, If I …, I'd …). C11 Build it together (6 min)

  • Job: "Our turn, all together, on one real case."
  • Feel: "with support, I can do it."
  • Teacher: shows the case and checks who did what; reads the job; the class tries each part (hold), then the model shows; everyone reads the whole message.
  • Reveal: the case → "Your job: …" → the notes and starters → each part: try (hold) → model.
  • MUST: one case everyone can see, from our world, with no new person without a bridge (25); a story that can only be read one way (66); a picture that tells the story, or words instead (69); the order case → job → hints (88); every model complete and polite (77, 78); simple enough for online, whole class (54).
  • MUST NOT: a confusing "sign" line that makes learners misread who called (66, 77); curt models (78); pair rules or many instructions (54).
  • FIXED: one shared case, notes → sentences, the model after each try.
  • VARIES: the case and the build format (ours: notes per part in E10; others may order notes, or build a Yes and a No version).
  • Changes with the formula: one try per formula part (three for P.P.F, four for O.R.E.O, the guide's loop for E04).

C12 The task (16 min)

  • Job: "Now for real: your classmates don't know your case. Make it clear to them."
  • Feel: "someone needs what I know, and I can tell if it landed."
  • Teacher: points to the cards; everyone chooses one and prepares (2 min); the teacher chooses one or two speakers per case; after each speaker the listeners answer the three questions; the speaker adds one missing detail if needed.
  • Reveal: the cards → step 1 → step 2 → step 3 → the goal line.
  • MUST: her sketch (67): picture cards (two daily life, two workplace) · three numbered steps: Choose & prepare · Warn the class · Did the message land? · the goal line at the bottom.

  • MUST NOT: a complex operation (55, 67); ours: no group rotation or phone switching (what the old version did).
  • FIXED: everyone prepares; the listeners do something with what they heard; the transfer to daily life and work is inside the cards (57) (E10: everyone prepares one case and the teacher chooses speakers; the form changes per lesson, see VARIES).
  • VARIES: the task type and its product, from the overview (13 types: briefing, timeline, decision board, four corners, walkthrough …), run whole-class.
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (overview part; E10 part): the group activity takes many forms, with simple instructions that take little time to give, for online and offline, for 1 to 12 learners; the teacher's view could offer other versions of the activity, chosen with buttons (G7).
  • Changes with the formula: the cards' notes are in the formula's parts.

C13 Push-back (7 min)

  • Job: "What if someone questions what you said? Don't stop at your point."
  • Feel: "I can keep my message when someone doubts it."
  • Teacher: reads the title; the case and the doubt; asks how they would answer; the class brainstorms with the tool (hold); shows the model; three doubts.
  • Reveal: title → the case and the doubt → the question → the tool (hold) → the model; doubts 2 and 3: the case → the question with the tool (hold) → the model.
  • MUST: her sketch (70): the heading and its line, the tool as boxes, the developed response, her footer line; three doubts, so there is interaction; a brainstorm with the tool before each model (70); one thing at a time (89); the tool connected to what came before and after (27) and easy to see (56).
  • FIXED: a doubt → a brainstorm with a tool → a developed response.
  • VARIES: the doubts and the tool, from the overview's bridge for the topic.
  • VARIES, from her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14 (overview part; E10 part): the tool is not only "because", which is too easy for Level 2 (Level 2 needs contrast, sequence, condition …), and its purpose is to clear the listener's doubt, not to practise a connector (G5). Ours: the tool is built from the lesson's own discourse (G11).
  • Changes with the formula: the bridge differs per topic (O07 "Aren't you worried?" · R11 "But a new one is so expensive." · N04 "Could it happen again?" · O02 repeat the other person's reason, then respond · E04 "It's not working").

C14 Try again and compare (6 min)

  • Job: "Now you have today's tools. If the same message came again, how would you answer?"

  • Changes with the formula: the toolkit shows the lesson's formula and language.

C15 Before → After, for the listener (3 min)

G The overview layer (her video walkthrough, 2026-10-14)

In her 38-minute video walkthrough the curriculum lead first goes through her TOUCH Level 2 CONVEY Overview v4 (0:20–24:40), then the live E10 page (24:50–38:30). Her main point: every lesson is built from all the elements of the overview, not by repeating the scam lesson on a new topic (A18).

How to read this part:

  • Two documents, two words (ours, to keep them apart): "the overview" is her Overview v4, one page of elements for the whole level; "the syllabus" is docs/reference/level2-syllabus-v1.5.json, the 48 topics with a field for each element (the field names are in brackets, e.g. fl).
  • Each element has four lines: What it is · Promised (what learners are told) · In a lesson (where its content comes from, and where it shows) · How it varies (from lesson to lesson).
  • What is promised: the four functions, O.R.E.O and P.P.F, and the Present / Past / Future time control are guaranteed across the 48 lessons (her overview's HOD coverage view: "Core progression across Level 2"). The banks, discourse, structures, tasks, application and evidence are "Curriculum design across lessons": learners are told what they will be able to do, as outcomes, not as lists, and the same outcome is not repeated too often (her video, overview part).
  • Why it can be promised now: before, learners booked lessons in any order and there was no fixed material, so nothing could be promised (her video, overview part).
  • Her coverage rule (overview part): each lesson has at least one function (two is fine) and O.R.E.O or P.P.F (an Optional organiser for a small share, G10). G12–G14 check the 48 against it.

G1 Functions & sub-functions

  • What it is: the four Communication Functions (Explain, Express Opinion, Narrate, Recommend), each with six Key Sub-functions, the "core communication moves" of the function (overview v4): 24 in all, ids E1–R6 (docs/subskill-tags.md).
  • Promised: every lesson trains at least one function, and across the 48 every function is met (12 lessons each).
  • In a lesson: its function (fn), its 3–5 sub-functions (subs) and those it borrows from another function (alsoUses). The goal page (C3) names the ability; the activities train the sub-functions (each screen's trains).
  • How it varies: mix different sub-functions from lesson to lesson, and build different activities for them (her video, overview part). Ours: six sub-functions are in 10 or more of their function's 12 lessons (G13, "frequent", e.g. State your opinion); the activity that trains them changes each time.

G2 O.R.E.O / P.P.F (the Core Formula Tool)

  • What it is: organisation support "used where it naturally fits" (overview v4): O.R.E.O for reason-based messages (an opinion, a justification, a reasoned explanation), P.P.F for change, development or progression over time.
  • Promised: every lesson uses O.R.E.O or P.P.F, except the small Optional share (G10); across the 48 both are met.
  • In a lesson: formula, and formulaUse (what each part is in this topic). Slot C9 names it ("The formula: P.P.F", A7); C11 and the task cards are written in its parts.
  • How it varies: the names and their use stay the same in every lesson; the keywords and the presentation change with the topic, so learners never feel they are doing the same thing again (her video, overview part). Her example: in a Narrate lesson, Past, Present and Future carry different things than in E10 (the parts as the syllabus gives them, N04: what happened and the fix → what we do now → if it happens again; E10: the case → how the trick works → what to do next time). The formula is reintroduced in every lesson, and the teacher's guidance matters (E10 part).

G3 Core time control

  • What it is: Present Time (facts, routines, current situations, opinions), Past Time (experiences, events, what happened), Future Time (plans, intentions, next steps), "revisited across Level 2" and "applied according to the communication goal and situation" (overview v4).
  • Promised: across the 48: Present in 48 lessons, Past in 27, Future in 35 (G13).
  • In a lesson: time and timeUse. Slot C10, with the names Past Tense / Present Tense / Future Tense (A7), and the toolkit on C14.
  • How it varies: the tense page must not look the same in every lesson; the grammar comes from sentences, not sentences from the grammar, with only a small explanation; E10's correction (do / don't, fix the red word) is one way among many (her video, overview part). Ours, other ways: sort the model's sentences by time; spot the change in a then-and-now pair; complete a timeline; choose between two forms in a reply.

G4 Functional language bank

  • What it is: purpose-based expressions for each function, in an Explain, an Opinion, a Narrative and a Recommendation bank (overview v4). The syllabus has 60 tools in five banks (its fifth: Open & connect), each a natural opening with this lesson's example.
  • Promised: an outcome ("you will practise how to …"), never a list of phrases (ours).
  • In a lesson: the three tools in fl (the tool, its frame, its example). They appear in the model (C8), the build (C11) and the toolkit (C14), and are highlighted where they are taught (ours: in bold, as E10's starters on C9 and C11; this is not the word-wall highlighting that A6 forbids).
  • How it varies: highlight the bank's tools, and do not reuse the same ones in every activity (her video, E10 part). A bank may come back in 2–3 lessons, and that is good, because repetition builds flexible use; later the tools of all 48 lessons can be collected for review (overview part). Coverage (ours: her 2–3 read as each tool in 2–3 lessons): every tool is in 2–4 lessons; eight are in 4 (G13).

G5 Connection & discourse support

  • What it is: language that helps learners connect, organise and develop ideas: reason, result, addition, contrast, sequence, condition, clarification, conclusion (overview v4), "selected according to the message flow".
  • Promised: an outcome (ideas connected more naturally), not a list of connectors.
  • In a lesson: the 2–4 groups in discourse (in links: the words and their job), taught inside the part that needs them (E10's own rule: never on a page of their own) and highlighted when taught.
  • How it varies: not only "because": it is too easy for Level 2, which needs contrast, sequence, condition and the others (her video, overview part and E10 part). A connector serves the listener (on the push-back: it clears the listener's doubt); it is not the thing being learned. Coverage: addition and clarification are in 2 lessons each, reason in 17, contrast in 19 (G13).

G6 Supporting language structures

  • What it is: structures that expand accuracy, flexibility and range: comparison, possibility, condition, ongoing situations, past experience, reporting, softening (overview v4), "integrated where they naturally strengthen the intended message".
  • Promised: not promised as a list; the syllabus v1.5 says the same (they are extra; what is promised is the time control).
  • In a lesson: the two entries in support, each with its purpose first (use), an example, and the grammar name only as a small tag (focus); taught on C10 with the tenses.
  • How it varies: each structure is there for a purpose (her examples: modals to soften the tone; comparison structures) (her video, overview part). Ours: the purpose is the heading ("To give a strong warning"), never the grammar name ("Imperatives").

G7 Communication task design

  • What it is: interactive task directions matched to the communication goal: explain · clarify · brief · report; share · discuss · justify · respond; reconstruct · update · share · reflect; compare · evaluate · advise · decide (overview v4).
  • Promised: an outcome (the learner speaks, listens, responds and follows up in a task), not a list of tasks.
  • In a lesson: task (its name, type, how and scale), on slot C12, run whole-class (A12); the push-back (C13) from bridge.
  • How it varies (her video, overview part and E10 part):
    • vary the scenes, the case studies and the interactive activities; creative, but a teacher can run it;
    • simple instructions that take little time; online and offline; 1 learner or 12;
    • perhaps a part that feels like a debate (ours: within the design brief's limit, §34, which keeps debate as a trained skill for Level 3);
    • a different warm-up in every lesson (C2), low pressure, because it is the first impression;
    • the first case (C4) is not always a phone call, and not every lesson starts with a listening;
    • the vocabulary page (C5): 3–4 versions are enough, more visual;
    • the group activity takes many forms; the teacher's view could offer other versions of an activity, chosen with buttons (ours: a teacher-only switch; the learners' screen shows the one chosen).

G8 Real-life application

  • What it is: varied adult situations across workplace and everyday life (overview v4).
  • Promised: an outcome: the same skill works in any role and in daily life.
  • In a lesson: transfer (one At work, one In life) and scenario; the task cards (C12: two daily life, two workplace) and the summary (C16).
  • How it varies: name the roles it helps (her examples: in accounts, a suspicious transaction; in marketing, a customer's concern) and daily life (her video, overview part and E10 part); a different role and a different everyday case in each lesson (ours).

G9 Evidence of progress

  • What it is: what developing communication can look like, three lines per function (overview v4). Planned from the end: outcome-based, something that could be shown to people outside the company as academically grounded (her video, overview part).
  • Promised: the outcome learners are told (C3) and see again at the end (C16).
  • In a lesson: evidence (three observable lines) and canDo; upgrade gives the before → after on C15. The teacher listens for the evidence in the task (C12) and the retry (C14) (ours: the teacher notes name the three lines).
  • How it varies: the same outcome is not repeated too often; every page has a purpose of its own (her video, overview part).
